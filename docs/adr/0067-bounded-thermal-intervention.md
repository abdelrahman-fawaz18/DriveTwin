# ADR 0067. Bounded cooling intervention in the thermal mission fixture

Use one fixed auxiliary cooling boost per eligible synthetic bus mission. The simulator models the boost as 120 W/K of additional housing heat transfer with a 1.2 kW auxiliary draw. It lasts at most 240 simulation seconds. Local load reduction takes precedence, engages at 354 K, and releases at 352 K. These values define a repeatable experiment. Vehicle calibration, pump sizing, hardware interlocks, and production access control are separate engineering work. [Intervention protocol](../../qualification/thermal/synthetic-intervention-protocol-1.0.0.json)

Bind the reservation to one simulation run and asset, the evidence tick, and the current process clock. Require the frozen two-probe advisory at reservation, grant, and first actuator use. The handoff expires at two simulation seconds or three real seconds, whichever is reached first. Both probes must belong to the current simulation tick, and the frame must be under two real seconds old. Save a one-use grant before exposing the cooling effect. A denied grant attempt consumes its reservation. A restarted fixture discards open reservations and active boosts while preserving spent grants and operator vetoes in its SQLite journal.

Use one simulated actuator owner for cooling and charging coordination. Local protection and an operator veto take precedence. A valid pending or active cooling action blocks a new diversion. An already selected diversion prevents a new boost. The Phase 4 diversion guard checks the route request, including its independent station and departure evidence and its own clock limits. A veto prevents future actions and cancels cooling. An already selected route is outside the veto's rollback scope. The qualification is local to the simulated boundary. It does not extend the Phase 4 automatic dispatch authorization or enable another write path in the monitoring services.

Compare local protection alone, threshold-based cooling, and predictive cooling using matched missions. Use a 60-second prediction extrapolated from the most recent 26 one-second motor readings. The forecast is assessed against future truth in local-protection-only traces and must meet error and coverage limits before the predictive controller can run. Treat this forecast and its interval as synthetic fixture evidence. The earlier physical testbench model remains a separate winding-temperature study. [Phase 5 forecast and actuation guide](../setup/synthetic-thermal-intervention.md)

For the actuator effect, replay the same simulated conditions and exact load-reduction schedule with the boost disabled, then measure motor temperature 120 seconds after grant. For the mission comparison, let local protection react independently in each run. Report temperature exposure, energy including the boost, arrival delay, and failure or incomplete counts. Freeze the protocol, source fingerprints, and development result before evaluating a disjoint reserved scenario set.
