# Charging requirements and evaluation rules

The charging advisor answers two questions. Can this bus reach and use a station while protecting its battery reserve? Among the stations that pass, which would interrupt service for the least time? For example, an open 150-kilowatt charger can lose to a 100-kilowatt charger if reaching it takes a long detour. The advisor records a recommendation for review. A diversion needs an authority check. An operator can request one explicitly, or the separately approved automatic profile can issue one in an isolated test from rest.

## Station and bus assumptions

The [versioned station catalog](../simulation/montreal/charging-stations-1.0.0.json) is a machine-readable list of three hypothetical sites. The [schema](../contracts/charging/station-catalog-1.0.0.schema.json) defines required fields and permitted values so software can catch a bad configuration before using it. A station has a map pin and a stop interval on a named, directed SUMO lane. SUMO, short for Simulation of Urban Mobility, represents roads by direction and lane. A stop interval measures meters from the start of that lane. This lets the simulator distinguish a nearby point on a map from a place the bus can approach and stop. The road geometry comes from the [reviewed OpenStreetMap extract](../data/public/montreal-core-v1/README.md). The three sites and their equipment are invented.

| Site | Power per charging bay | Connector labels | Charging bays / waiting places | Station status age limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `depot-a` | 150 kW | `sim-dc-plug-a` | 1 / 2 | 30 simulated seconds |
| `opportunity-b` | 100 kW | `sim-dc-plug-a` | 1 / 0 | 15 simulated seconds |
| `west-c` | 75 kW | `sim-dc-plug-a`, `sim-dc-plug-b` | 2 / 1 | 30 simulated seconds |

A bay is one charging position. The initial bus uses the synthetic plug label `sim-dc-plug-a`. The labels let us exercise connector compatibility without assuming a real charger standard. The catalog assumes independent rated power per bay, 95% transfer efficiency, and synthetic waiting places managed by the scenario. Waiting places represent queue capacity. They do not establish any physical parking layout. Status and queue estimates come from timestamped scenario inputs that are available at the decision time. The catalog provides limits and capacity, so it can be shared by scenarios with different outages and queues.

## D-12 decision rules for the simulated advisor

State of charge (SoC) is usable energy divided by the assumed usable battery capacity. The starting reserve is **10%** of usable capacity, consistent with Phase 2 advisory displays. For a 350-kWh model that is 35 kilowatt-hours (kWh). The separate 20% informational warning still means the operator should pay attention. A warning grants no diversion permission. The design uses 25% as a provisional post-charge target for the bounded comparison scenarios. Verify that the target is within local charge limits and sufficient to finish the remaining mission with reserve. Review these assumptions with an operator or domain reviewer before using them outside the local simulator. [Phase 2 advisory assumptions](setup/advisory-mission-assessments.md) · [Bus parameters](../simulation/montreal/bus-parameters.json)

1. **Decide whether to advise.** Require admitted live simulation inputs for the same asset and run, including current location, usable battery energy, and a supported route and forecast horizon. Use the existing five-second wall-clock input freshness limit for live forecasts. Consider diversion when the conservative minimum energy on the remaining mission falls below the reserve. A missing or out-of-domain forecast produces an unavailable decision. A bus already below reserve triggers a degraded-state alert.
2. **Filter before ranking.** Require a legal directed path to the stop and back to an admissible remaining mission segment. Keep mandatory stops and their order. Check connector compatibility, operating access, an available bay or an admissible bounded wait, and sufficient energy throughout the approach and expected wait. Treat the station status as stale when its age reaches its configured limit. Compute age on the same simulation run clock and reject timestamps ahead of the decision. A known outage, full station with no waiting space, or stale or missing status excludes the candidate. Use a conservative estimate of minimum energy over the drive and waiting period. A candidate passes that check only if the estimate is at least the reserve. Sensitivity bounds from Phase 2 are provisional inputs for offline exploration until arrival accuracy is measured.
3. **Rank by added mission time.** Use seconds for all terms. Add the extra travel time through the station relative to the original route to the same rejoin point, expected wait, and estimated charge time. Clamp negative extra travel time to zero. For an initial constant-power estimate, divide the battery energy needed in kWh by net charging power in kW and multiply by 3,600 to get seconds. Net power is the smaller of charger and bus acceptance limits, adjusted for transfer losses and auxiliary demand. A nonpositive net rate or missing qualified estimate makes that calculation unavailable. Model slower charging near the target once the selected power profile is qualified. Break exact score ties by station ID. Record all score terms, exclusions, input and model references, and assumptions.
4. **Reconsider a selected station only when needed.** Immediate loss of feasibility, such as an outage, invalidates a pending recommendation. For two still-feasible choices, require a sustained improvement and a cooldown before switching. This is hysteresis, a rule that prevents small status changes from flipping the choice back and forth. Set and freeze the improvement threshold, persistence, and cooldown during offline development before final comparison. Every replan uses only information available by that decision time.
5. **Respect authority and report failures.** The local protective limits and an authorized operator override govern any future diversion. Revalidate the route, charge and station status before command handoff. The plan must fit the allowed mission segment and timing. When no station passes, return `no_feasible_station` with reasons and alert the operator. Allow continuation only if the existing mission remains within its qualified limits. Test a separately qualified controlled-stop or safe-hold path for an energy-critical case. A depletion, abort, or unresolved action stays visible in the mission result.

## Scenario and evidence rules

A baseline is a simpler comparison method run under the same scenario conditions. Compare the proposed advisor with nearest feasible station by directed road distance and with SUMO's built-in Stationfinder. Use the same mission inputs, station capacity and closures, admitted observations, initial battery energy, and protective limits. Record which policy has control. Enable Stationfinder only in its own arm. Disable towing and any silent battery reset, and score removal or depletion as a failure. If an arm cannot be configured for equivalent visibility or protection, report the mismatch and keep the matched-comparison gate open. [SUMO Stationfinder](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Simulation/Stationfinder.html) · [SUMO battery and charging stations](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Models/Electric.html)

| Planned case | What the result must show |
|---|---|
| Normal diversion | Arrival at the intended stop, actual charging power and energy gain, correct target detection, and resumed mission progress. Tie every independently observed effect to its command ID. |
| Incompatible, unreachable, stale, or reserve-violating station | One explicit exclusion reason per failed constraint. Include a case where all stations are excluded and the advisor returns `no_feasible_station`. |
| Outage, full bays or changing wait | Decisions use only status available at the time. Loss of feasibility invalidates the plan. Minor fluctuations leave the station choice stable. |
| Unexpected energy use or depletion | Record the reserve violation or depletion. A stopped or removed vehicle counts as incomplete. Report an alert and the declared fallback. |
| Override, stale inputs or expired handoff | The simulated controller respects authority, freshness, local protection, and command deadlines. No command acknowledgment counts as effect evidence by itself. |

For station-arrival qualification, run independently collected approaches to each station. Capture energy immediately before departure and at the stop **before charging** and keep the minimum usable energy along the drive. Include the expected stationary load in cases with a wait. Seal predictions from the admitted pre-drive inputs before scoring them against the plant readings. Use development drives to set numerical error and range-width limits, then freeze them before running separate reserved drives. Report energy and SoC error, interval coverage and width, and false-feasible decisions where a recommendation would have violated reserve. A false-feasible case blocks dispatch qualification in the claimed domain. Retain all failed, unavailable, and out-of-domain cases. The earlier known-route Phase 2 study is separate from this approach evaluation. [Reference drives and protocol](setup/station-arrival-reference-drives.md)

For each policy comparison, retain the configured scenario, seed, road/catalog/policy/model fingerprints, input times, station status, decisions and exclusion reasons, command history, independent telemetry, and cleanup. Report completion over all attempted missions, reserve violations, depletion, delay, energy delivered to the bus, replans, and operator interventions. Keep the underlying traces private and record sanitized aggregates in Git. Phase 4B verifies offline eligibility, ranking, and status-driven recommendation changes. Phase 4C verifies the isolated nominal missions and adverse cases. Phase 4D verifies authenticated charging and compares the policies with common physical station power at departure and the same reserve cutoff. Occupancy and delayed status are covered by separate offline tests. The approved automatic profile applies to one parked synthetic bus.
